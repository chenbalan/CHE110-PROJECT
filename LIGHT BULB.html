How to Safely Handle and Dispose of Light Bulbs

  • Step 1: Identify the bulb type. Compact Fluorescent Lamps (CFLs) and fluorescent tubes contain mercury and require special handling. LEDs and regular incandescents do not contain mercury but should still be kept out of standard recycling.
  • Step 2: Never toss in curbside recycling. Light bulb glass is made from different material compositions than jars or bottles and will ruin a batch of standard glass recycling.
  • Step 3: Pack carefully to avoid breakage. If storing spent bulbs for a special collection drop-off day, keep them in their original boxes or wrap them carefully to prevent shattered glass hazards.
  • Step 4: Look for retail drop-offs. Many national home improvement retailers offer free collection bins at the front of their stores specifically for CFL bulbs and rechargeable batteries.
Mercury Alert: If a CFL bulb breaks in your home, do not vacuum it. Open a window to ventilate the room for 15 minutes, scoop up fragments using stiff paper or cardboard, and wipe the area clean with damp paper towels.
